# niche

thesis: keeberia does not sit in one niche, it sits at the intersection
of several, and the intersection is the actual product. anne's brainstorm
lists below, kept close to verbatim (source: screenshots, sept 26 2026).

or simply put: nobody wants "a keyboard design tool" on its own, but a lot
of people want tactile stim toys, keyboard culture, desk aesthetics, and
customizable accessories, and keeberia is one product that gives them all
four at once.

## what keeberia hits

- tactile stim toys
- mechanical keyboard culture
- desk setup aesthetics
- customizable accessories
- tiny cute objects

## because it hits

- creator economy
- personalization
- hardware tooling automation
- custom manufacturing
- desk setup culture

## what users can do (the pitch)

- drag modules around
- customize layouts
- choose aesthetics
- map macros/hotkeys
- generate production-ready boards

which is exactly the kind of tactile productivity object people would
obsess over.

## examples anne pointed at (sept 26 2026)

these back the niche claims above with real reference photos, not just
the pitch bullets:

- **capsmith is the "tiny cute objects" + "personalization" bullet made
  literal**: a croissant with almond shavings glued onto a Tab-adjacent
  key, a burger topper on a macro key. capsmith is picking toppings for
  your keyboard, see engines/capsmith/README.md.
- **clicketies are the "tactile stim toys" + "creator economy" bullet**:
  a chocolate-toned 3x3 keycap block on a keychain clip, and a single
  frog-face keycap on its own keychain clip, both fidget objects, no
  typing function. see offerings/clicketies/README.md.
- **licensed-character toppers exist in the wild** (a Kuromi-shaped
  topper glued onto an F1 key): shows the topper axis can go all the way
  to full character sculpts, not just food and simple motifs. licensing
  for recognizable characters (sanrio etc.) is a real constraint capsmith
  would have to solve for before offering anything character-branded,
  not something to route around quietly, flagged here as an open question
  for whenever capsmith's topper library gets designed.

## note

anne separately confirmed keeberia's two product modes read as
"functional" (macropads, keyboards, typing hardware) vs "fidget"
(clicketies), a cohesive split worth keeping in mind, but not written
into offerings/README.md as its own heading yet, to avoid it competing
with or muddying the existing macropads/keyboards/keycaps/clicketies/
cases/types list. logged here, not promoted to product docs.
