# commercial tools: the funded end of the space

> thesis: commercial eda tools (flux.ai, easyeda and its spark program,
  the big-name edas) solve adjacent problems with different economics.
  they matter to keeberia as positioning evidence, not as engineering
  references: what they automate, what they charge, and what they still
  make people do by hand.

## questions for the phase 15 dispatch

- flux.ai: ai-assisted eda, pricing model, what the assistance actually
  does (note: keeberia's no-ai-in-copper rule is a positioning contrast
  here, state it as a contrast, not a claim of superiority)
- easyeda + jlc's ecosystem lock-in: free eda welded to one fab, the
  opposite of keeberia's fab-agnostic stance
- any configurator-style commercial products (custom keyboard company
  configurators) and where they stop

deliverable: evidence-backed page + a row in the parent comparison table.
