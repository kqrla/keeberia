# v2 CJK

> thesis: CJK input is IME country, not legend country. chinese types hanzi through a pinyin IME over qwerty, japanese through romaji entry on standard keyboards, korean through the dubeolsik layout that puts consonants left and vowels right. the caps-engine question is what legends buyers want aesthetically, not what input requires.

## evidenced facts

- pinyin: latin letters represent chinese syllables, an IME window lists matching hanzi, works on a qwerty board `[evid-llog-024]`
- japanese input predominantly uses romaji phonetic entry on standard qwerty letter keys `[evid-leg-010]`
- dubeolsik (korean) arranges consonants left, vowels right `[evid-leg-009]`
- jis physical boards exist and differ: 109 keys, single-unit backspace, shortened spacebar `[evid-lay-015]`
- hanzi legend scale on physical caps (strokes per glyph) is the manufacturing lift, already covered by the legend process lines: doubleshot tooling per glyph `[evid-leg-001, evid-leg-002]`, dye-sub as the low-tooling path `[evid-leg-003]`

## open questions

- cantonese (v2.0's second step): cantonese IME is fuzzy-phonetic with no fixed romanization, which may make cantonese-legend demand differ from mandarin. no corpus line beyond the input-method fact.
- zhuyin/bopomofo (taiwan) and cangjie: input methods with different board implications. taiwan is on the market ladder but its input line is not excerpt-backed yet.
